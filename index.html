About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical Engineering at NC State, where I work with Prof. Yuan Liu on hardware-native instruction sets for hybrid qudit–oscillator quantum computation on trapped-ion hardware. Most compilers assume a register of qubits, while a trapped-ion processor natively offers multi-level electronic states and the motional modes of the ion crystal; I build instruction sets that expose that richer hardware to algorithms instead of hiding it behind a qubit abstraction. I also work on quantum signal processing, both its robustness to control error and its photonic realization, and on pulse-level control and compilation, mapping logical operations onto the laser and RF drives an ion trap actually executes. Earlier, across two master’s degrees, I studied MEMS/NEMS strain characterization of 2D TMDC materials and single-electron transistors.

My dissertation is on the co-design of quantum algorithms, architectures, and control for early fault-tolerant hardware. I expect to defend in May 2027 and am on the academic job market for positions starting Fall 2027.

Research

Quantum algorithms are designed against an idealized machine. Quantum hardware is built without much reference to the algorithms that will run on it. The cost of that separation appears in the compiled circuit, where a useful computation expands into far more operations than the device can execute within its error budget. I co-design the algorithm, the architecture, and the control together, and I measure the result in gate counts and error suppression.

Hardware-aware quantum signal processing

Quantum signal processing expresses a wide class of algorithms as a single polynomial transformation, applied by interleaving one fixed operation with tunable rotations. The standard analysis assumes those rotations are exact. I study what the protocols do when they are not. Block-encoding error and phase-angle error cross-compensate to first order, and a constrained optimizer built on that structure suppresses correlated control error by 4 to 19 times. With collaborators in photonics I co-led the first experimental realization of QSP on a programmable photonic integrated circuit, synthesizing nonlinear functions on linear-optical hardware.

Papers: Block-encodings and polynomial transformations · Robustness-aware QSP · Universal function approximation on a photonic platform

Hardware-native hybrid architectures

A trapped-ion processor carries more than two levels per ion, and the ions share a set of motional modes. Compiling to qubits throws most of that away. I design instruction sets whose primitives are the interactions the hardware already performs. Three primitives of the trapped-ion qudit–oscillator set generate universal Hamiltonians with no synthesized mode-coupling operation, and a Toffoli gate follows in a single step from the native interactions. Compiling the dissipative vibronic dynamics of a three-chromophore complex onto one 171Yb+ ququart costs 28 gates per Trotter step against 96 for the superconducting realization, and reproduces the published trajectories to roughly 1.1% RMSE.

Papers: A native trapped-ion hybrid qudit–oscillator instruction set

Hardware-aware quantum control

Every logical gate on an ion trap is a shaped laser or radio-frequency pulse. I build the layer that turns a specified operation into those pulses. HyPulse is a GRAPE-based synthesis framework for hybrid CV–DV qubit–oscillator gates, published at IEEE QCE 2026. On top of it we assembled a reusable library of compound pulse gadgets for Hamiltonian simulation. FACT, a fidelity-aware compiler for trapped-ion machines, is under review at HPCA 2027. I also extended HybridLane, a PennyLane extension, to native qudit–oscillator circuits.

Papers: HyPulse · Compound pulse gadgets · FACT · Learned pulse synthesis

Nanophotonics and quantum materials

I work on the devices that hold and carry quantum information. This covers inverse-designed non-periodic circular cavities for spin–photon interfacing, optimized by genetic algorithm, and circular gratings designed through guided-mode expansion, both published at SPIE in 2025. During my master's work at the MonArk NSF Quantum Foundry I designed and fabricated MEMS/NEMS platforms for optical strain studies of 2D materials, from electron-beam lithography through SEM and AFM metrology. That device experience sets what I am willing to let an instruction set assume about its hardware.

Papers: Inverse design of non-periodic circular cavities · Optimization of circular gratings
